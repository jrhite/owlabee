PACER docket alert options compared

PACER

PACER is the federal judiciary's own access service and the source every other option draws on. Its fee page: "$0.10 per page", "You won't be charged more than $3 per document", no maximum for search results, non-case-specific reports or transcripts, "$2.40 per audio file", and "Users are billed on a quarterly basis if they accrue more than $30 in the previous quarter" (PACER: How fees work). PACER is where you read the documents; it is not an alert service.

Court RSS feeds

Many district and bankruptcy courts publish an RSS feed of new docket entries from CM/ECF, free and without a login, but each court decides whether to publish one and which entry types it carries. Our own probe of every court's feed (district and bankruptcy courts on 2026-10-17, appellate and national courts on 2026-11-07) found: district courts 42 full, 22 partial, 17 empty, 13 that did not answer from a datacenter IP; bankruptcy courts 52 full, 19 partial, 6 empty, 17 no answer; courts of appeals 6 partial and 7 empty. The court-by-court table is on PACER RSS feeds by court.

CourtListener docket alerts

Free Law Project's CourtListener sends docket alerts by email or webhook "when there are new filings in cases" (CourtListener help: Alerts). Its help page says it allows a number of docket alerts for free, gives a bonus to users with the RECAP browser extension, lets members "create as many docket alerts as they need" while monitoring for commercial usage, and offers bulk commercial rates; it also says alerts "are only as good as our sources", and that for less active cases they may take longer "or may not arrive at all" (CourtListener help: Docket Alerts for PACER). For a person following a handful of cases, this is the best free option.

PacerMonitor

PacerMonitor's pricing page lists Plus at $49 a month (single user, documents 15¢ a page, 5 custom alerts), Professional at $99 a month (documents 10¢ a page, 20 custom alerts), Enterprise at $499 a month (15 users, unlimited custom alerts), an API by quote, and a free account at a flat $5 per document (PacerMonitor: Plans and Pricing).

Our Actor: PACER RSS Docket Monitor

PACER RSS Docket Monitor reads the courts' public RSS feeds, normalizes each entry (court, case number, case type, entry text, document link, filed time) and filters by party, case number, case type, entry type, keyword or bankruptcy chapter; monitor mode returns only entries not seen on earlier runs, with an optional webhook. On November 8, 2026 the Store API listed $0.005 per court feed checked, $0.003 per matching entry and $0.001 per entry when no filter is set. It sees only what the feeds publish: no documents, nothing from courts with empty feeds, and older filings only through an optional CourtListener pass with your own CourtListener token.

Where the alternatives are better

  • Following a few specific cases: CourtListener docket alerts are free up to its limit and draw on more sources than RSS alone.
  • Reading documents: PACER (or a service that resells its documents, such as PacerMonitor) is required; RSS feeds and our Actor give the entry and a link, not the PDF.
  • Courts without a full feed: a commercial service that polls dockets will see entries that a partial or empty feed never publishes.
  • Our Actor fits when you want every new entry matching a filter across many courts, as structured JSON in your own pipeline, on a schedule.
