Public Tenders API: EU TED, UK Find a Tender and AusTender

The EU, the UK and Australia each publish public procurement notices through an open API, with different query models and field names. This Actor searches all four sources and returns every notice in one record shape with the deadline, estimated value, buyer, classification codes and links to the notice and its documents, and on a schedule returns only notices you have not seen.

The source and its limits

Source idSourceWhat it returnsKeyword searchLicense
tedEU TED (Tenders Electronic Daily): above-threshold procurement in the 27 EU countries, the EEA and partner countriesCalls for competition, award and direct-award notices, prior information noticesTED full-text search, on the serverCC BY 4.0 (Commission Decision 2011/833/EU)
findATenderUK Find a Tender: UK above-threshold noticesTender, award and planning notices (OCDS)Matched by the Actor in the date windowOpen Government Licence v3.0
contractsFinderUK Contracts Finder: central government and below-threshold noticesTender, award and pipeline notices (OCDS), with the "suitable for SMEs" flagMatched by the Actor in the date windowOpen Government Licence v3.0
ausTenderAusTender: Australian Commonwealth contract noticesContract notices (awards) with value, dates and UNSPSC codeMatched by the Actor in the date windowCC BY 3.0 AU

All four are official open APIs that need no key or account, and the Actor uses no login, proxy or browser. The UK and Australian APIs have no keyword parameter, so the Actor reads their date window once per run and keeps notices whose title, description, buyer name or code descriptions contain every word of the keyword. countries narrows TED; the UK and Australian sources are national.

The record shape matches the opportunity records of the Grants.gov API Actor (opportunityId, title, agency, postedDate, closeDate, noticeType, classifications, estimatedValue), so US and international results can go into one table. Every record and the run summary carry the source's license and attribution line; keep it with any redistribution.

Limits worth knowing

  • AusTender's open approaches to market are not in its open API, so AusTender returns awarded contracts only.
  • TED reads up to maxPagesPerSource pages of 100 notices per keyword (default 10). The shared UK and Australian windows read at most 3 Find a Tender pages, 5 Contracts Finder pages or 7 AusTender days per keyword in a run; the run summary says when a cap was hit.
  • Lots, procurement documents and submission links for TED come from one more request per notice, only with fetchDetails on.
  • Buyer contact persons are left out by default (omitContacts), and award winners whose names read as private individuals are withheld with nameWithheld: true. Company winners are always named.

Use cases

Daily tender alert for a sector

Search a CPV branch such as 72000000 (IT services) with keywords: ["*"], turn on onlyNewSinceLastRun, and schedule it daily with a webhookUrl.

UK opportunities for small suppliers

Search findATender and contractsFinder together; Contracts Finder notices carry its "suitable for SMEs" flag.

Who won Australian Government contracts

Search ausTender with a UNSPSC code such as 80100000 (management consulting) to see recent awards, suppliers and values.

FAQ

Does it need an API key or proxies?

No. All four sources are official open APIs that need no key or account.

Why do UK and Australian notices take longer to search?

Those APIs have no keyword search, so the Actor reads the date window and matches the keyword itself. Narrow the window or the codes to see more notices within the page caps.

Can I get only new tenders every day?

Yes. Turn on onlyNewSinceLastRun and schedule the task. The first run is a baseline; later runs return only new or changed notices, flagged isNew or isUpdated. Use a different stateStoreName per schedule.

Why are buyer contact names missing?

Contact persons are personal data under the GDPR and UK GDPR, so omitContacts is on by default. Turn it off to get the contact as the notice publishes it; you are then responsible for processing it lawfully.

Can I combine this with US federal opportunities?

Yes. The record shape matches the Grants.gov and SAM.gov opportunity records, so the datasets can be merged.
